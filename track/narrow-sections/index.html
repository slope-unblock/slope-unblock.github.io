Narrow Track Sections

Narrow sections reduce your correction budget, so enter already stable, avoid unnecessary edge movement and save small corrections for alignment.

⚡
Quick answer

Narrow track rewards restraint. Enter from a stable line, avoid unnecessary recentering and use the smallest correction that keeps the ball inside the available width.

Solve the entry before the track becomes narrow

If you arrive at the thin section already carrying lateral motion, there may be no room to cancel it. Use the wider approach to settle the line first.

Stop steering when the line is already safe

Many narrow-section crashes come from correcting a position that would have survived. Once the ball is on a usable path, let it travel instead of trying to perfect the center.

Look at the exit

A narrow lane may widen, turn or lead directly into another feature. Knowing the exit tells you whether to stay neutral or begin a gentle shift before the width returns.

Do not use edge saves as a standard technique

Recovering from the edge can happen, but a strategy that requires repeated last-second rescues is unstable. Improve the approach so the narrow section starts with more margin.

Apply Narrow Track Sections in a normal run

Turn the explanation into an observable test: keep the setup stable and change only the decision this page is asking you to improve. For Narrow Track Sections, keep the first visible principle in mind: Solve the entry before the track becomes narrow. Then use stop steering when the line is already safe as the second checkpoint, so the test covers both the initial choice and what follows from it.

Keep score in the background during the test. Note entry position, amount of steering and landing stability first. A single high number can come from a favorable route, while a lower-scoring attempt can still reveal a cleaner technique that is more likely to repeat.

The final measure is option value: did the move leave enough width, time and visibility for the next decision? That standard remains useful even when the exact track changes, which is why it is a better practice metric than memorizing one screenshot or route sequence.
