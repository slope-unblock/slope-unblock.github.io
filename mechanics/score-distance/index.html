Score and Distance

The Slope score is tied to continued progress through the endless course, so survival is the central scoring mechanic.

⚡
Quick answer

Slope score rises with successful progress through the run, so score is closely tied to how long you survive and how far you travel. The actionable part is therefore consistent survival.

Score is an outcome, not a steering input

There is no separate scoring button to optimize. Every line choice affects score indirectly by deciding whether the run continues. A safe landing that preserves control is often more valuable than a risky maneuver that looks faster.

Use score to compare similar conditions cautiously

Generated routes differ, and hosted versions may expose different leaderboard services. Compare your own trend across multiple runs rather than treating one number as a universal measure of skill.

Pair score with a cause note

After a run, add one short label such as late gap read, oversteer, poor landing or technical interruption. Over several attempts, the combination of score and cause reveals whether practice is actually reducing a failure pattern.

Records need context

Any claim about a highest score should identify the version, date and evidence. A screenshot from an old provider board should not be presented as a permanent world record without current verification.

Apply Score and Distance in a normal run

Make the advice measurable by choosing a single moment in the run where it should improve entry, steering or exit quality. Repeat the test on several route shapes. The technique should adapt to gaps, turns, ramps or open track without becoming a rigid script. The cue is useful when it improves the decision while allowing the exact amount of steering to match the visible geometry.

After each run, move backward from the final crash to the earliest decision that reduced your safe options. That point is the next training target. If the same cue improves that moment across several attempts, keep it; if not, rewrite the cue in terms of a clearer visual trigger.
