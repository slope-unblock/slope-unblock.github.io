Slope Score Milestones: Better Goals Than One Record

Use process milestones—clean landings, fewer edge falls and calmer high-speed steering—to support higher Slope scores.

⚡
Quick answer

Slope milestones are most useful when they describe a skill you can reproduce, not an arbitrary number. Combine score targets with process goals such as cleaner landings or fewer emergency corrections.

Start with consistency milestones

Before chasing a large score, aim for several runs that avoid the same common mistake. Repeating a stable opening is evidence that the underlying control habit is improving.

Add route-reading goals

A useful milestone might be recognizing gaps before they become urgent or completing several transitions with a stable exit line. These goals stay meaningful even when generated track layouts differ.

Use score as a secondary marker

When the process becomes more reliable, your typical score should rise. Track the range of several runs so one lucky attempt does not hide an unchanged failure pattern.

Increase difficulty one layer at a time

After an early-game skill is stable, test it under higher speed. Do not replace every goal at once; keep one known habit while adding a harder visual or timing requirement.

Use an early, wide section to verify the basic response, then look for the same decision later when speed is higher. Do not demand identical key-hold times; the track changes. What should stay consistent is the order of the decision: recognize, choose a line, steer, release and reassess.

The final measure is option value: did the move leave enough width, time and visibility for the next decision? That standard remains useful even when the exact track changes, which is why it is a better practice metric than memorizing one screenshot or route sequence.
