How to use this section

Slope strategy is about preserving options

Useful Slope strategy starts before the ball reaches the danger. Read the route, choose a corridor, use the smallest steering action that can reach it, and avoid leaving the ball with unnecessary lateral carry. This makes the next decision easier instead of solving one obstacle at the expense of the next.

Slope tips become valuable only when they include conditions. “Stay centered” is useful on open track, but not when the next opening clearly requires an early side commitment. “Tap instead of hold” is useful for fine correction, but a wider shift may need a longer input. Every recommendation should answer when it works and when it stops working.

Turn mistakes into a strategy map

Classify crashes as late reading, excessive correction, poor landing line, edge drift, wrong risk choice or technical input failure. After several runs, repeated categories reveal which guide is worth practicing next.

🧠

Strategy cue

Do not ask “Where is the center?” first. Ask “Which position gives me the most safe options after the next feature?”

Slope strategy is about preserving options: enter hazards with room, shorten inputs as speed rises and judge a dodge by the exit line it creates.

Practical takeaways: Slope Strategy

The practical test for “Slope Strategy” is whether it improves the next position, not whether the current movement looks aggressive. Read one feature ahead, choose the smallest correction that creates a usable line, then release long enough to see the effect before stacking another input.

Apply “Choose a focused lesson” and “How to use this section” conditionally. Wide track can reward patience, while a narrow landing can require an earlier commitment. The point of a strategy page is to give you a decision rule and its limits, not a move that must be repeated regardless of the geometry.
